# Transformation Grammar

The two abstract panels begin from the same evidence but take opposite spatial paths. Build each plan as a mapping:

`output form → exact source evidence → transformation operation → retained identity or spatial role`

No major form should lack this mapping.

## Upper panel — aggregating reconstruction

Ask: if most secondary information vanished and only identity, contour, direction, volume, and essential character remained, what would survive?

The upper should be concentrated, complete, stable, identity-bearing, and relatively monumental or totemic. It is not required to use geometry, relief, dots, collage, or any predetermined surface language.

### Method

- Select three to five strongest identity anchors.
- Preserve their basic adjacency and orientation; do not arbitrarily scatter originally connected structures.
- Compress complexity into the source-appropriate mix of point, line, plane, silhouette, color field, repeated unit, simplified curve, or structural facet.
- Allow enlargement, recentering, contour emphasis, environmental compression, deletion of secondary detail, merging of similar forms, and conversion of local texture into rhythm.
- Retain one to three environmental proofs as a frame, base, boundary, background field, real silhouette, or simplified structural line.
- Keep the environment subordinate to the subject or core space.

The subject is purified, not dismantled. Even without the middle photograph, the viewer should roughly recognize the category or core image.

### Avoid

- painterly or geometric filters;
- blur, texture, or mosaic presented as transformation;
- generic abstraction unrelated to identity;
- invented sun, moon, birds, rings, mountains, planets, flowers, or symbols;
- abstraction so aggressive that identity disappears;
- the same composition mechanism planned for the lower panel;
- shrinking the subject into a reusable field of template-like empty space.

## Lower panel — dispersing reconstruction

Ask: if the subject and environment were separated into visual components and released from their original position, scale, and physical ownership, what new space could they generate?

The lower should be open, decentralized, spatial, migratory, and more surreal, while remaining source-accountable.

### Method

Separate the source into point, line, mass, contour, texture, environment, and identity components. Operations may include displacement, enlargement, reduction, elongation, interruption, repetition, dilution, gathering and dispersal, direction change, proportion mismatch, spatial reassignment, and depth reordering.

Choose transformations from real source forms:

- point genes may become paths, density changes, rhythm sequences, floating particles, distance, or time traces;
- line genes may become routes, flows, cracks, wind, motion, divisions, or connectors;
- mass genes may become terrain, structural sections, platforms, layers, clouds, large color fields, or occluding collage forms;
- contour genes may become islands, ridges, openings, boundaries, torn edges, or abstract silhouettes.

These are logical possibilities, not required results. Select only what the current source supports.

Preserve at least two or three traceable identity anchors: a key contour, direction, action, local structure, rhythm, color relation, or subject-environment distance. They may move or change scale, but may not all disappear.

### Negative space

Design one primary open region. Derive its position and shape from real sky, water, wall, shadow, fog, road, or another open area in the source. Do not distribute fragments evenly. Let some components float, cross, extend, recede, or vanish.

The viewer should first see a new surreal field, then discover that it is built entirely from the middle photograph.

### Surrealism without invented objects

Create surrealism through scale change, displacement, spatial ownership exchange, local texture becoming macro-space, small elements becoming routes, fixed structures floating, subject forms entering the environment, environmental forms entering the subject, or one source shape holding multiple meanings.

Do not add unrelated moons, eyes, doors, ladders, planets, birds, or fantasy objects.

### Avoid

- another complete centered subject;
- a recolor or weaker duplicate of the upper panel;
- moving the intact subject without transforming relationships;
- indiscriminate duplication or equal fragmentation;
- stock cosmos, map, mountain, landscape, or collage language;
- clutter that destroys source traceability;
- surreal objects unsupported by the source.

## Absolute difference between panels

| Upper | Lower |
| --- | --- |
| aggregation | dispersion |
| inward concentration | outward release |
| subject priority | spatial-field priority |
| stronger center | decentralization |
| wholeness | displacement and fragmentation |
| identity purification | identity migration |
| environment compression | negative-space expansion |
| local enlargement | proportion mismatch across parts |

They are two paths from the same photograph, not two abstraction levels on one path. The upper preserves the subject's form; the lower releases the subject's field.

## Color

Derive all major colors from the middle photograph: deepest, lightest, subject primary, environment primary, shadow, highlight, and small accent. Brightness, saturation, transparency, temperature, and area proportion may change, but do not introduce a strong hue absent from the source.

If the source is muted, remain muted. If it contains a clear accent, preserve it without expanding it until the source's color identity changes.

Never default to ivory art paper, vintage green, gold, seal red, Morandi palettes, blue-orange cinema color, or black-gold styling unless the source contains them.

## Material

Derive material behavior from the source:

- stone → mineral grain, abrasion, roughness;
- metal → oxidation, reflection, scratching;
- water → transparent layers, flow edges;
- plant → fiber, veins, organic diffusion;
- architecture → wall, glass, concrete, structural line;
- cloth → folds, flexible surface, fiber;
- fog or sky → gradient, translucency, air.

Do not apply one painterly texture to every source. Material supports form; it never replaces structural reasoning.

## Continuity and abstraction level

Unify panels through source direction, color relation, contour, subject orientation, environmental boundary, or a repeated group of points, lines, and fields. Do not force a literal line through all three.

Target perception:

- upper: recognize the subject first, then notice its abstraction;
- middle: clearly see factual origin;
- lower: see a new space first, then discover the hidden source genes.

The final relationship is: middle provides fact, upper distills image, lower releases relationships.
