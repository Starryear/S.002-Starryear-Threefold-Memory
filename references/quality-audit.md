# Quality Audit

Inspect the final assembled artwork at full view and panel level.

## Source audit

- Can every major upper and lower form be linked to exact source evidence?
- Did any generic decoration, habitual symbol, or unsupported fantasy object appear?
- Were subject count, identity, and important relationships preserved?
- Do all major colors come from the source?
- Is material behavior derived from the photographed matter or environment?

## Middle evidence audit

- Is the actual uploaded photograph used rather than a generated imitation?
- Are faces, species, objects, orientation, perspective, and spatial direction unchanged?
- Was resizing proportional and cropping minimal?
- Is any grading slight enough that the original remains factual photographic evidence?

## Difference audit

- Does the upper truly aggregate around identity or the core spatial structure?
- Does the lower truly disperse components into a new field?
- Do the panels use opposite spatial mechanisms?
- Is the lower more than a recolor, relocation, or degraded copy of the upper?

## Recognition audit

- Without the middle, is the upper's subject category or core scene still legible?
- Does the lower retain at least two or three discoverable identity anchors?
- With the middle restored, can a viewer trace the abstract decisions?
- Do all three panels belong to the same source world without becoming simple copies?

## Composition audit

- Is hierarchy clear?
- Does the lower contain purposeful source-derived negative space?
- Are fragments unevenly distributed rather than tiled everywhere?
- Is decoration subordinate to structure?
- Has abstraction preserved the source's most important relation?

## Corrective priority

If upper and lower are too similar, revise the lower first:

1. move identity components away from their original location;
2. break the complete silhouette while retaining two or three anchors;
3. enlarge source-derived negative space;
4. widen scale differences;
5. release a local texture as a path, density field, or distance sequence;
6. convert a subject, object, or architectural component into environment;
7. remove repeated centered composition.

If the upper loses identity, restore adjacency, silhouette, orientation, or one stronger anchor rather than adding detail everywhere.

If unsupported motifs appear, remove them; do not justify them after the fact.

Retry only the failing generated panel and reassemble. Normally stop after at most two targeted retries.
